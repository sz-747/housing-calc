How Dominus works

Dominus compares the financial outcome of renting vs buying a Sydney property over your chosen time horizon

What it works out

You enter your income, savings, deposit, how long you plan to stay, and the suburb you are looking at. Dominus then runs both paths forward year by year.

On the buying side it adds up the deposit, stamp duty, LMI, mortgage payments, and ownership costs, and tracks how much of the property you actually own as the loan shrinks and the price grows.

On the renting side it works out the rent you pay each year, and grows your deposit as if you had invested it instead of buying.

At the end it tells you which path leaves you wealthier, by how much, and the year buying overtakes renting.

Why it's useful

"Should I rent or buy?" is a hard question because the costs are hidden and spread over many years. Stamp duty and LMI hit you upfront, the mortgage drags on for decades, and the deposit you spend on a house is money you could have invested instead.

Dominus puts all of that in one place and gives you a clear number for your own situation, instead of a rule of thumb. Change the suburb, deposit, or how long you stay and you can see straight away how the answer moves.

A question to consider

Which leaves you wealthier at the end of your horizon: owning a property or renting and investing your deposit instead?

Buy wealth = equity in your property minus all cash spent (deposit, stamp duty, LMI, mortgage payments, annual ownership costs).

Rent wealth = the invested value of your deposit minus all rent paid over the horizon.

Key terms

Stamp duty is a one off tax the NSW government charges when you buy a property. The more expensive the place, the more you pay, and it is due upfront on top of the price.

LMI (Lenders Mortgage Insurance) is a one off fee the bank makes you pay when your deposit is under 20% of the price. It protects the bank if you cannot repay the loan, not you, but you are the one who pays it.

Deposit is the cash you put in at the start. The bank lends you the rest. A bigger deposit means a smaller loan and, past 20%, no LMI.

Equity is the share of the property you actually own. It is the current value minus what is still owing on the loan, so it grows as you pay the loan down and as the property rises in value.

Mortgage is the loan you take to cover the rest of the price. You pay it back over the loan term with interest, so the total you repay is well above what you borrowed.

Ownership costs are the ongoing bills that come with owning, like council rates, strata, insurance, and maintenance. Renters do not pay these.

Opportunity cost is the gain you give up by spending your deposit on a house instead of investing it. If you rented, that money could have grown in the market, so Dominus counts that missed growth as a real cost of buying.
